The Sony HT-S100F is an entry-level 2.0-channel television soundbar rated at 120 W total power output. According to Sony Australia, the unit incorporates an S-Master digital amplifier, an integrated Bass Reflex speaker, and S-Force Front Surround virtual audio processing. Rather than using multiple separate components, the single enclosure measures 900 mm across and weighs 2.4 kg, housing the stereo speakers in an all-in-one format. Physical inputs include one HDMI ARC output, one optical audio input, and a USB Type-A port for music playback, alongside a Bluetooth 4.2 receiver for wireless pairing.

## Who it suits

This soundbar suits television viewers looking to improve built-in TV dialogue and basic stereo audio without managing separate rear speakers or a floor-standing subwoofer. Because it connects using a single HDMI ARC cable, users whose televisions support HDMI Consumer Electronics Control (CEC) can operate power and volume with their existing TV remote. It also suits renters or users in apartments who want to avoid running extra speaker wires or vibrating floors with a dedicated sub unit. Conversely, buyers seeking a multi-room setup, voice assistant control, or immersive spatial audio should look elsewhere. The unit lacks network connectivity and does not decode formats like Dolby Atmos or DTS:X, remaining limited to Dolby Digital and two-channel LPCM.

## Australian notes

According to Sony operating manuals, the HT-S100F is designed for 220 V to 240 V AC power at 50 Hz or 60 Hz, matching standard Australian mains electricity, and comes with a detachable power cable. Sony rates operational power consumption at approximately 30 W, dropping to 0.5 W or lower in standby mode. Australian retailers including JB Hi-Fi, Harvey Norman, and Sony Australia list the product with a standard one-year manufacturer warranty. Because the soundbar completely lacks Wi-Fi hardware, it does not support smart home integrations such as Matter, Apple Home, Google Home, Alexa, SmartThings, or Home Assistant. Any wireless streaming is strictly local over Bluetooth 4.2 using the basic SBC codec. Those planning to wall-mount the soundbar in a rental property should verify their tenancy agreement regarding wall fixings before drilling.

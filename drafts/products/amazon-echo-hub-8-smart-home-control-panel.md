The Amazon Echo Hub (model B0BCR4YW51) is an 8-inch wall-mountable smart home control panel designed to serve as a central touch interface for connected equipment. According to Amazon, the device features a 1280 x 800 touchscreen, top-ported stereo speakers, and a passive infrared (PIR) proximity sensor that detects when someone approaches the screen. It doubles as a multi-protocol smart hub, incorporating an onboard Matter Controller, Thread Border Router, Zigbee radio, and Bluetooth Low Energy Mesh alongside dual-band Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz). Amazon notes that the panel maintains local device control for compatible equipment during internet disruptions, while also displaying live camera feeds from external security cameras.

## Who it suits
This panel suits households that rely on Amazon Alexa as their primary smart home platform and want a physical, dedicated wall display for lighting, locks, and compatible camera feeds. It is practical for users managing mixed-protocol networks, as the onboard radios eliminate the need for separate standalone bridges for Zigbee or Thread devices.

Conversely, it does not suit homes built around Apple Home, Google Home, or Home Assistant, as retailer and manufacturer documentation confirms the interface is locked exclusively to Amazon's operating system. Those looking for an interactive video calling station should also look elsewhere; review listings from PCMag Australia note that the Echo Hub lacks a built-in camera. Users prioritising room-filling music playback may also need to pair external Bluetooth speakers, as the internal speakers are tuned primarily for chimes and Alexa voice responses.

## Australian notes
The Echo Hub is widely available through Australian retailers including JB Hi-Fi, Bing Lee, Officeworks, and Amazon Australia, and comes with a 12-month manufacturer warranty. The unit is powered by an included 12.5 W USB-C power adaptor with a 1.8-metre cable. For a cleaner installation without visible surface cords, Amazon notes it supports Power over Ethernet (PoE) using a third-party PoE-to-USB-C adapter compliant with IEEE 802.3at PoE+. If you plan to run cabling through wall cavities or install recessed power points behind the mounting bracket, check your state or territory's electrical safety regulations and licencing requirements. Renters should note that wall-mounting hardware requires surface drilling, while the tabletop stand is sold separately. Basic dashboard functions do not require ongoing subscriptions, though optional plans apply for cloud video storage from brands like Ring or Blink.

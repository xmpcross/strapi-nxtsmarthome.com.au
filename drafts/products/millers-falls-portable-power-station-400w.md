The Millers Falls PPS400W, distributed in Australia by TWM Imports Pty Ltd, is a portable lithium power station designed for off-grid power and mobile device charging. According to retailer listings from Harvey Norman and Ballarat Trailers & Towbars, the unit incorporates a 93,600 mAh lithium battery (rated between 346 Wh and 366 Wh) paired with a pure sine wave inverter. It delivers 400 W of continuous power with a 450 W peak surge capacity. Hardware connections include two 240V AC sockets, two Quick Charge 3.0 USB ports, two USB Type-C ports, and four DC 5521 sockets, monitored via an integrated LED control panel.

## Who it suits

This unit suits campers, travellers, and households looking for an independent power reserve to run low-draw electronics during outages or outdoor trips. Its 400 W continuous threshold provides sufficient output for small electronic items such as laptops, smartphones, portable fridges, CPAP machines, and camping LED lights. The battery is rated for 1,000 recharge cycles, according to distributor specifications.

Conversely, anyone looking to run high-draw heating elements, coffee machines, electric kettles, or demanding workshop power tools should skip this unit, as these will trigger its 450 W overload cut-off. Smart home users should also note that the PPS400W features no Wi-Fi hardware, Bluetooth capability, or companion smartphone app. It cannot be integrated into platforms such as Home Assistant, Apple Home, or Google Home, operating purely as a standalone appliance.

## Australian notes

The PPS400W is sold through Australian machinery retailers and department stores, backed by a 12-month manufacturer express warranty through TWM Imports. The unit comes equipped with two standard Australian 240V AC sockets and includes a 240V AC wall charger alongside a 12V DC vehicle lead. Recharging takes between 8 and 10 hours from empty, whether using mains power or a compatible 12V car or solar input. Solar panels are not included in the box and must be purchased separately.

For Australian households planning emergency power setups, check device wattage ratings before connecting them to portable inverters. When storing large lithium battery units indoors or in vehicles, check your state's electrical safety regulator guidelines for safe storage and handling procedures.

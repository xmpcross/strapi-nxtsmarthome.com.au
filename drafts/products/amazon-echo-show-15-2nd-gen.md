The Amazon Echo Show 15 (2nd Gen) is a wall-mountable 15.6-inch smart display designed to serve as a visual command centre and entertainment screen for the home. According to Amazon, this second-generation hardware introduces an upgraded 13 MP centred camera with auto-framing and 3x zoom (replacing the 5 MP sensor from the original model), alongside improved dual speakers with boosted bass. The screen runs 1080p Full HD resolution and integrates Fire TV software, shipping with an Alexa Voice Remote so household members can browse supported services like Netflix, Prime Video, and YouTube. For home automation, Amazon integrates local hub radios supporting Matter, Thread, and Zigbee, paired with Wi-Fi 6E connectivity, while retaining physical controls including a sliding camera shutter and microphone-disconnect button.

## Who it suits
This device suits busy households already committed to the Amazon Alexa ecosystem that want a shared digital bulletin board. Placing it on a central kitchen or hallway wall allows family members to check shared calendars, to-do lists, sticky notes, and weather widgets at a glance. It also suits users seeking an all-in-one smart home hub to control compatible Zigbee, Thread, or Matter smart bulbs, plugs, and switches without needing a separate bridge.

It is not suited to homes running on Apple Home or Google Home, as it operates entirely within the Alexa and Fire TV environment. Renters or homeowners who do not want to drill into walls and do not wish to purchase the optional desktop stand should look at smaller tabletop alternatives.

## Australian notes
Amazon supplies the Echo Show 15 (2nd Gen) with an Australian-compliant 30W power adapter featuring a 1.5-metre lead, backed by a 12-month manufacturer warranty through local retailers. The unit supports Wi-Fi 6E across 2.4 GHz, 5 GHz, and 6 GHz bands, maintaining backward compatibility with standard 2.4 GHz home Wi-Fi networks. Streaming availability relies on Australian Fire TV app availability, with support for major services including Prime Video, Netflix, and YouTube.

Renters should note that the included wall-mount bracket requires drilling into the wall; check your tenancy agreement and relevant state or territory residential tenancy laws regarding wall fixtures before drilling. Furthermore, if you use the camera for indoor home monitoring or drop-in features, confirm that you comply with your state or territory's surveillance device laws regarding recording conversations and activities in private residences.

The TP-Link Tapo L530B is an A60-format smart LED globe fitted with a standard Australian B22 bayonet cap. According to TP-Link Australia, it delivers up to 806 lumens of light (equivalent to a standard 60-watt incandescent globe) while drawing between 8.3 and 8.7 watts of power. It connects directly to your home Wi-Fi network without requiring a dedicated hardware hub, providing access to 16 million colours, tunable white light between 2,500 K and 6,500 K, and in-app dimming from 1% to 100%.

## Who it suits

According to manufacturer specifications, this globe suits homeowners and renters seeking colour-changing lighting without the added cost of a dedicated gateway or bridge. Because it uses a B22 bayonet cap, it fits standard light sockets found throughout Australian properties, making installation as straightforward as changing a standard globe. It suits users who rely on Google Assistant or Amazon Alexa for voice commands, while TP-Link documentation confirms it can also link to Samsung SmartThings and Home Assistant via third-party settings. Households seeking scheduled timers, away-from-home occupancy simulation, or circadian colour adjustments can manage these through the Tapo app without any recurring subscription fees. However, buyers invested in the Apple Home ecosystem or newer Matter-based setups should look elsewhere, as the L530B lacks native support for HomeKit and Matter.

## Australian notes

The L530B is designed for Australian mains electricity, running on 220–240 V at 50/60 Hz with a standard B22 base. TP-Link Australia provides a two-year limited manufacturer warranty for its smart lighting range, which applies alongside your consumer guarantees under Australian Consumer Law. Because the bulb communicates exclusively over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (802.11b/g/n), modern dual-band Australian home Wi-Fi routers must have an active 2.4 GHz band broadcast during setup; 5 GHz connections are not supported. TP-Link documentation highlights several operational restrictions: the globe cannot be paired with traditional in-wall dimmer switches, as electrical dimmers cause performance issues. If you intend to alter existing hardwired dimmers or light switches, check with your state's electrical safety regulator regarding licensed electrical work. Additionally, the bulb is rated strictly for indoor, dry environments and is not suitable for damp rooms, outdoor fittings, or totally enclosed fixtures.

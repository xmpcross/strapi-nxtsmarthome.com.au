The Sonos Ray is a compact standalone soundbar designed for secondary televisions, bedrooms, or smaller living spaces. Measuring 557 mm wide, it features forward-facing acoustic drivers—comprising two tweeters, two midwoofers, and four Class-D amplifiers—built to project audio forward without side-firing reflections, allowing it to sit neatly inside enclosed media console shelves. TV connectivity relies entirely on a digital optical audio input, meaning it does not include HDMI ARC or eARC ports. For wireless audio, the Ray operates over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and supports Apple AirPlay 2, Spotify Connect, and the Sonos app ecosystem, though it omits Bluetooth audio and built-in voice microphones.

## Who it suits

This soundbar suits apartment dwellers, bedroom TV setups, or secondary entertainment spaces seeking improved speech clarity and TV audio without a large footprint. Viewers who frequently struggle to hear dialogue benefit from Sonos's Speech Enhancement and Night Sound modes, which boost vocal frequencies and reduce loud sound effects for late-night viewing. It also suits existing Sonos multi-room audio owners who want a straightforward addition to group with other rooms for music streaming, or who plan to add rear surround speakers and a Sub Mini down the track.

Conversely, households with newer televisions that only offer HDMI outputs should skip the Ray, as connecting it requires a compatible optical audio output on the TV. Anyone wanting Dolby Atmos spatial audio or HDMI-CEC control will need to look towards higher-tier options like the Sonos Beam (Gen 2).

## Australian notes

In Australia, the Sonos Ray ships with a standard 2-metre mains power cable (100–240 V, 50/60 Hz) and a 1.5-metre optical audio cable. Wi-Fi streaming connects exclusively via 2.4 GHz 802.11b/g/n networks or its 10/100 Mbps Ethernet port; ensure your home router does not isolate 2.4 GHz smart home traffic.

Because the Ray relies on an infrared (IR) sensor to adjust volume with your TV remote, television remotes that rely exclusively on Bluetooth or RF communication must be configured to emit IR signals, or volume must be adjusted through the Sonos app. Sonos Trueplay room tuning software remains exclusive to supported iOS devices; Android owners cannot calibrate the acoustics directly with their phone. Sonos provides a manufacturer warranty (check the term with your retailer), and the consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law apply alongside it.

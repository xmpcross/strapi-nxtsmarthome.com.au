The TP-Link Tapo P110M Mini Smart Wi-Fi Plug is an indoor power adapter that adds scheduling, remote switching, and electricity consumption monitoring to standard Australian wall outlets. According to TP-Link Australia, the plug connects directly to home Wi-Fi and features Matter certification, allowing cross-platform operation with major smart home assistants without relying solely on proprietary software. The physical housing measures 76.5 × 43.5 × 42 mm and features flame-retardant casing, a manual control button, and internal child safety shutters.

## Who it suits

This smart plug suits Australian households looking to monitor energy usage on specific appliances and integrate controls into multi-platform setups. Because it carries Matter certification with Multi-Admin support, TP-Link notes it fits households running Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, Samsung SmartThings, or Home Assistant simultaneously. TP-Link confirms that no ongoing subscription is required to view energy statistics, configure auto-off timers, or establish automated schedules.

It also suits renters because it requires no fixed electrical modifications; you plug it straight into an existing mains point. However, households seeking to control heavy-duty machinery or high-demand heating equipment should look elsewhere. TP-Link rates the unit for a maximum load of 10 A and 2300 W, which excludes high-draw appliances or dedicated 15 A circuits. Those without a dedicated Matter hub who want local cross-platform automations will also find their integration options restricted to the Tapo application.

## Australian notes

TP-Link Australia supplies the Tapo P110M with a standard Australian 3-pin plug and passthrough socket rated for 220–240 V at 50/60 Hz. The manufacturer confirms the plug is certified under Australia's Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) and includes a two-year local warranty under its Smart Power policy.

Network connectivity relies strictly on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (IEEE 802.11b/g/n), though Bluetooth is included to streamline initial pairing. Users with combined 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz routers may need to ensure their network allows devices on the 2.4 GHz band during setup. Because it is intended strictly for indoor environments between 0 °C and 40 °C, TP-Link notes it should not be deployed in exposed outdoor outlets or high-humidity settings. If you plan to install permanent sub-circuits or dedicated smart wall outlets instead of plug-in adapters, consult a licensed electrical contractor to ensure compliance with Australian standards.

The Meross Smart Wi-Fi Plug Mini AU (MSS210) is a compact smart adapter designed to automate standard Australian electrical appliances. It plugs directly into a wall outlet and connects to a home network over 2.4GHz Wi-Fi, removing the requirement for a separate proprietary hub. Through the Meross mobile app or connected voice assistants, users can create automated on and off schedules, configure auto-off countdown timers, and toggle power remotely.

## Who it suits

This smart plug suits Australian renters and homeowners seeking simple switching automation for floor lamps, desk fans, chargers, and small domestic appliances. Because it plugs directly into an existing power point, it requires no electrical modifications and is easy to pack up when moving.

It also suits multi-ecosystem households. Meross lists broad platform support, including Apple HomeKit, Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant, and Samsung SmartThings, alongside community compatibility with Home Assistant. Anyone building an automated home across mixed operating systems will appreciate this out-of-the-box cross-platform flexibility.

However, buyers looking for power usage analytics should avoid this model. Although some retailer descriptions group it under energy-monitoring ranges, Meross official technical specifications confirm that the MSS210 AU does not contain energy monitoring hardware. Those who require active wattage tracking or solar diversion automations should look instead at models with dedicated metering, such as the Meross MSS310.

## Australian notes

The MSS210 AU is configured for standard Australian three-pin sockets (Type I). Manufacturer documentation specifies an input of 220–240V at 50/60Hz and a maximum current output of 10A, supporting appliances up to 2400W. Meross also confirms that the device carries Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) and SAA electrical certifications, ensuring it meets Australian electrical safety standards.

In terms of physical fit, Meross lists the body dimensions as 6.7 cm by 6.7 cm by 5.12 cm (excluding pins). While labelled as a 'Mini' plug, its square body can overlap adjacent switches or neighbouring plugs on compact horizontal double power points found in many Australian homes.

For connectivity, the plug relies on standard 802.11 b/g/n 2.4GHz Wi-Fi bands. It does not support 5GHz frequencies or enterprise Wi-Fi setups, so routers with combined network names may need separate 2.4GHz SSIDs during the initial pairing process.

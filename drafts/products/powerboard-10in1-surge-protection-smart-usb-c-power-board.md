The 10in1 Surge Protection Smart USB C Power Board is a compact desktop and wall-mountable power board designed to supply up to ten devices from a single wall point. Australian retailer specifications indicate that the '10-in-1' layout comprises four standard three-pin AC sockets and six USB ports (five USB-A and one USB-C). While some marketplace titles designate the board as 'smart', manufacturer and retailer descriptions reveal this refers strictly to an integrated auto-detect charging chip that manages current distribution to mobile devices, rather than wireless home automation. Physical protection includes built-in surge suppression, an overload cut-off switch, internal safety shutters on the AC sockets, and a master power switch.

## Who it suits

This board suits home offices, workbenches, and bedside setups where multiple low-power USB peripherals—such as wireless earphones, smartwatches, and phones—need to charge alongside computer monitors or lamps. The rear keyhole mounting slots are helpful for renters and homeowners looking to clear floor space by mounting the unit against a skirting board or under a desk.

It is not suitable for users seeking a genuine smart-home power strip. Because it lacks Wi-Fi, Zigbee, Bluetooth, or Matter radios, it cannot integrate with Apple Home, Google Home, Amazon Alexa, or Home Assistant, and it does not support remote app scheduling or energy monitoring. Buyers requiring high-speed laptop charging should also consider alternatives, as the total shared USB output tops out at 17W (3.4A), which is insufficient for USB Power Delivery laptop charging.

## Australian notes

The power board is fitted with Australian Type I sockets and a 10A plug designed for standard 230V/240V 50Hz mains power, rated for a total maximum load of 2,400W. When buying power boards through online marketplaces, check the physical housing for the Regulatory Compliance Mark (RCM) triangle to verify compliance with Australian electrical standards (AS/NZS 3105).

Avoid overloading the 10A threshold by keeping high-draw thermal appliances (such as space heaters or kettles) off shared boards. For fixed electrical upgrades or adding extra wall points in your home, consult a licensed electrician and check your state's electrical safety regulator. Ensure you keep your store receipt to access standard statutory guarantees under Australian Consumer Law.

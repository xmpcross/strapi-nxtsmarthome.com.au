While listed in retailer databases as a Bing Lee air conditioning controller, Bing Lee is an Australian electrical retailer rather than an original equipment manufacturer. The retailer does not market a proprietary controller; instead, listings under this name refer to third-party controllers stocked by Bing Lee, specifically Sensibo hardware such as the Sensibo Sky and Sensibo Air Pro. According to manufacturer documentation, these standalone devices connect to a home Wi-Fi network and use an infrared transmitter to mimic standard air conditioner remotes. This allows users to add smartphone app management, schedules, and temperature automation to older split systems, window-mounted units, or portable air conditioners without replacing the cooling unit.

## Who it suits
This hardware setup suits Australian homeowners and renters seeking to modernise an existing cooling system that currently relies on a standard infrared remote. Per the brand, it allows you to configure 7-day timers, set room humidity and temperature triggers via Climate React, and issue voice commands using Amazon Alexa or Google Home. It is also suitable for users who want to avoid replacing functional cooling systems simply to gain smart scheduling.

Conversely, it does not suit households with ducted systems that lack an infrared remote receiver. It will also frustrate users who regularly use their original wall or handheld remotes, as research notes confirm the infrared transmitter cannot track manual state adjustments made outside the mobile app. Additionally, each indoor air conditioner head requires its own dedicated controller, which may become impractical for multi-room installations.

## Australian notes
When bought through Australian retailers like Bing Lee, these third-party units come backed by Australian Consumer Law alongside a standard 12-month manufacturer warranty. For power, the devices use a 5V USB connection paired with an Australian plug, running entirely off standard mains power points without requiring hardwired installation from an electrician. For renters, plug-in infrared units allow smart climate scheduling without altering wall fixtures, though tenants should check their state tenancy authority guidelines if mounting accessories or using specific appliances.

Networking is limited to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi bands (802.11 b/g/n), so dual-band home routers must support this frequency. Platform integration depends on the hardware stocked: Sensibo Sky integrates with Google Home and Amazon Alexa, while Sensibo Air Pro adds native Apple Home support; Matter compatibility is not confirmed. While basic schedules and remote operation are free via the companion app, the manufacturer notes that advanced energy analytics, graph histories, and automated geofencing require a paid Sensibo Plus subscription.

The Netatmo Smart Thermostat (model NTH01) is a connected climate controller designed for residential heating systems, including compatible heat pumps, hydronic boilers, and pellet stoves. Designed in collaboration with Philippe Starck, the unit features a minimalist e-Paper screen that displays the target and ambient room temperatures. The hardware consists of two components: a battery-powered thermostat unit operating on three AAA batteries, and a wireless relay that wires directly into the heating appliance. Netatmo states that the thermostat communicates locally with its relay via radio frequency, while the relay connects to home Wi-Fi on the 2.4 GHz band to enable remote scheduling and automation through the Netatmo Energy or Home + Control app.

## Who it suits
According to Netatmo, the thermostat suits homeowners operating individual hydronic boilers, compatible heat pump systems, or heating units configured with low-voltage dry-contact (volt-free) switching terminals. It is a practical fit for households in cooler Australian climates—such as Victoria, Tasmania, or the ACT—where hydronic slab or radiator heating is installed. Users invested in major smart home ecosystems will appreciate that Netatmo supports Apple HomeKit, Google Assistant, and Amazon Alexa, allowing temperature adjustments and status checks through unified apps and voice commands.

However, households relying on standard Australian ducted reverse-cycle air conditioning or wall-hung split systems should skip this unit. It lacks the multi-stage 24 V heating and cooling terminals required by standard ducted units and does not feature infrared transmitters for split-system heads. Renters should also look elsewhere unless their property already features an identical plug-and-play thermostat socket, as installation typically requires modifying fixed heating controls.

## Australian notes
Before choosing the Netatmo Smart Thermostat in Australia, buyers must confirm heating system compatibility. Many Australian central heaters, such as ducted gas heating systems, use proprietary digital wall controllers or complex multi-wire thermostat interfaces that are not compatible with Netatmo's basic switching relay. Netatmo provides an online compatibility tool to verify wiring requirements prior to purchase.

The wireless relay connects directly to 230 V mains power and heating control wiring. Fixed mains wiring is generally work for a licensed electrician; check your state's electrical safety regulator for local requirements. Ensure any imported kit carries appropriate Australian regulatory compliance marks (RCM) and includes hardware compatible with Australian 230 V 50 Hz power standards. For connectivity, the relay operates on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (802.11 b/g/n) and does not support 5 GHz networks, though Netatmo confirms basic heating schedules and manual setpoints continue to operate if internet connectivity drops out.

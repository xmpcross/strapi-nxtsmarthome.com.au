The Dreame Matrix10 Ultra (model RLX95CE) is a robot vacuum and mopping system designed for automated floor maintenance. According to Dreame Australia, the unit provides up to 30,000 Pa of suction alongside a dual-roller detangling brush. Its central feature is a multi-mop switching dock that automatically alternates between three specialised mop pad sets to prevent cross-contamination across varying surfaces. The dock also incorporates 100°C hot-water pad cleaning, hot-air drying, and a 3.2 L dust bag. For navigation, Dreame states the robot uses a retractable LiDAR turret that lowers body height to 89 mm under furniture, combined with AI obstacle avoidance trained on more than 240 object types.

## Who it suits
This robot suits larger properties with varied floor finishes where manual mop pad swapping is inconvenient. Dreame specifies that the ProLeap suspension can negotiate single vertical steps up to 40 mm and stepped dividers up to 80 mm, making it suitable for homes with raised timber floor transitions or tiled split levels. Households with pets may benefit from the detangling brush system and self-emptying base. It is also well suited to automated setups requiring Matter, Apple Home, Google Home, or Home Assistant compatibility, with no subscription fees required for standard app controls.

Those living in compact apartments may want to skip it due to the physical footprint. The dock requires clear clearance and weighs 16.6 kg unloaded. Buyers seeking simple maintenance should also note community reports of mechanical jams within the motorised mop-changing tray if pads shift.

## Australian notes
Stock sourced from official Australian retailers—including JB Hi-Fi, The Good Guys, and Bing Lee—includes a standard Australian 220–240V plug cord, with the dock rated at 120 W power consumption. Local purchases are supported by a 3-year manufacturer warranty. Due to the carton dimensions and total weight, Dreame notes that official shipments cannot be delivered to Australia Post PO Boxes. The vacuum connects exclusively to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi bands, which means Australian homes using unified dual-band mesh routers may need to ensure 2.4 GHz broadcasting is enabled during initial setup.

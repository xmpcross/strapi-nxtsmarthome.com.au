The TP-Link Tapo L530E is an A60-form-factor smart LED bulb fitted with an E27 Edison screw base. According to TP-Link Australia, it produces an output of 806 lumens, matching a 60-watt incandescent globe, while drawing between 8.3 W and 8.7 W of power. The globe features full RGB colour capability with 16 million shades, alongside an adjustable white colour temperature spanning 2500 K to 6500 K and dimming control from 1% to 100%. Because it connects straight to home Wi-Fi, it does not require a separate hardware hub or a paid subscription to operate.

## Who it suits
This bulb suits homeowners and renters who want lighting automation through Amazon Alexa, Google Assistant, or Samsung SmartThings. Because it installs into existing light fittings without any wiring changes, it is particularly suitable for renters who cannot modify built-in hardware. It works well for spaces where you want to schedule lighting routines or change the ambience between warm white, cool daylight, and colours using the Tapo mobile app.

However, households invested in the Apple ecosystem should note that native Apple HomeKit is not supported, with functionality limited to Siri Shortcuts. If you require Matter support for cross-platform local management, TP-Link confirms this older model lacks Matter capability. It is also unsuitable for rooms with physical in-wall dimmer switches, as TP-Link states the bulb requires a constant electrical current and must only be dimmed digitally.

## Australian notes
In Australia, the Tapo L530E is rated for standard 220–240 V, 50/60 Hz electrical supplies. It uses an E27 screw base, common in domestic lamps and pendant fittings across Australia, though buyers should verify whether their fixture requires an E27 screw or a B22 bayonet fitting before purchasing.

Network connectivity is strictly confined to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi (802.11b/g/n); it will not connect to 5 GHz networks, meaning dual-band routers may require temporary band separation during setup. For warranty coverage, TP-Link Australia lists a two-year manufacturer warranty period for smart lighting on its official support page, though some retailers such as Officeworks advertise a 12-month warranty. If you plan to replace in-wall light switches to prevent power cuts to the globe, consult a licensed electrician and check your state's electrical safety regulator.

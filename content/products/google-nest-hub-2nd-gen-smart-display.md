The Google Nest Hub (2nd Gen) is a compact desktop smart display built around the Google Assistant ecosystem. According to Google specifications, the device pairs a 7-inch 1024 × 600 LCD touchscreen with a 43.5 mm full-range speaker, three far-field microphones, and an integrated Soli radar sensor. Because it operates without a physical camera, user interaction occurs through voice commands, touch navigation, or radar-powered Quick Gestures. It serves as a visual control interface for Google Home devices, displays Google Photos albums, streams media from supported services, and monitors bedside rest.

## Who it suits
This display suits households invested in Google Home that need a central dashboard to control lights, view connected camera feeds, and check daily schedules. Because Google omitted a camera, the device is well suited to bedrooms and private areas where camera lenses are undesirable. Google notes that its Soli radar tracks sleep stages, ambient light, and breathing movement from a bedside table without requiring wearable trackers. It also suits households building out Matter smart home setups, as Google confirms the display functions as both a Matter controller and a Thread border router.

Buyers who rely on Amazon Alexa or Apple Home should bypass this unit, as it does not natively integrate with competing voice platforms. Users wanting two-way video calling will also find it unsuitable, as the hardware cannot send outbound video feeds. Additionally, Google confirms that long-term access to the Sleep Sensing tool requires a paid subscription after the preview period concludes.

## Australian notes
Units distributed through official Australian retailers include a 15 W external power adapter equipped with a standard Australian mains plug and a 1.5-metre cable. For local Wi-Fi networks, the display includes dual-band Wi-Fi 5 capable of connecting to standard 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz bands. It requires continuous mains electricity and does not include an internal battery.

Google provides a one-year consumer hardware limited warranty in Australia, which applies alongside statutory consumer guarantees under the Australian Consumer Law. If you rent or share a home, check your state or territory tenancy guidelines regarding the placement of internet-connected microphone devices in shared areas.

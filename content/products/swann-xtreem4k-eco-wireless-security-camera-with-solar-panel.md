The Swann Xtreem4K Eco Wireless Security Camera with Solar Panel (model SWIFI-4KXTRMECO) is an outdoor surveillance camera designed for wire-free perimeter monitoring. According to technical specifications from Swann Australia, the camera records video in 4K Ultra HD resolution across a 120-degree field of view. It is powered by an internal 6,500mAh rechargeable lithium battery that connects to an included 3W outdoor solar panel via a 4-metre USB-C charging cable. The unit uses True Detect heat and motion sensing to trigger recording, which works alongside an integrated spotlight to deliver colour night vision up to 10 metres, or infrared black-and-white night vision up to 15 metres. An included 32GB MicroSD card handles on-device recording, supplemented by rolling one-day cloud storage without requiring a monthly fee.

## Who it suits
This camera suits homeowners and property owners who need outdoor coverage in areas lacking mains power. Because the solar panel features a 4-metre lead, users can position the solar collector in direct sunlight while mounting the camera beneath shaded eaves or porch areas. It also suits users looking to avoid ongoing subscription costs, as event recording functions locally out of the box. 

However, households requiring 24/7 continuous video recording should bypass this camera, as Swann Support notes that it only records motion-activated clips to conserve battery life. It is equally unsuitable for smart home enthusiasts relying on Apple Home, Matter, or local Network Video Recorders, as it lacks local RTSP streaming and desktop viewing software.

## Australian notes
The camera connects solely to 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi networks (802.11 b/g/n), meaning Australian households with unified dual-band Wi-Fi setups may need to ensure the 2.4 GHz band is accessible during initial pairing. Third-party voice control is limited to Amazon Alexa and Google Assistant. Australian retailers including Officeworks and Harvey Norman list the camera with a two-year manufacturer warranty, and the hardware features an IP56 weather-resistance rating against dust and rain.

Renters planning to mount the camera and solar panel should review their tenancy agreement and consult their state residential tenancies authority before drilling fixing brackets into exterior walls. Furthermore, ensure camera positioning respects neighbouring properties by checking your state or territory surveillance devices and privacy legislation regarding residential video capture.

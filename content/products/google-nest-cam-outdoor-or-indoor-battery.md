The Google Nest Cam (Outdoor or Indoor, Battery) is a wireless 1080p HDR security camera designed for indoor or outdoor residential monitoring. According to Google specifications, it features a 130-degree diagonal field of view, night vision up to 6 metres via six infrared LEDs, and an IP54 weather-resistance rating. It operates on an integrated 6 Ah lithium-ion battery, connects via dual-band Wi-Fi (2.4 GHz and 5 GHz), and is managed through the Google Home app. Google states that on-device processing recognises people, animals, and vehicles locally, delivering smart alerts and three hours of rolling event video clips without requiring an ongoing service contract.

## Who it suits

This camera suits homeowners and renters with existing Google Home or Amazon Alexa ecosystems who want flexible placement without hiring a tradesperson to run electrical cabling. Because Google provides core detection features and three hours of event history out of the box, it suits households seeking event-driven security without compulsory monthly fees. However, buyers wanting continuous 24/7 video monitoring should look elsewhere unless they intend to run permanent power, as Google restricts 24/7 recording to wired setups paired with a paid Nest Aware Plus plan. Smart-home setups built around Apple Home or Home Assistant should also bypass this unit, as Google does not list compatibility for those platforms.

## Australian notes

Google lists an operating temperature ceiling of 40°C and an IP54 rating. In parts of Australia subject to extreme summer heat or heavy downpours, mounting the camera under eaves or shaded areas will help keep temperatures within specification and reduce direct exposure. 

The camera package includes an Australian-compliant 7.5W USB-A power adapter and an indoor-rated one-metre magnetic charging cable. If you intend to power the camera outdoors continuously, Google requires a separate outdoor-rated weatherproof cable. Local retailers list manufacturer warranty coverage between 12 and 24 months, which sits alongside your statutory rights under the Australian Consumer Law.

Renters should consult their tenancy agreement and local residential tenancies authority before drilling mounting fixtures into external walls. Additionally, if positioning the camera near boundaries, footpaths, or neighbouring properties, check your state or territory surveillance device and privacy laws regarding residential recording.

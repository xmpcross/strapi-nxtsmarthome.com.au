The Ring Video Doorbell Pro 2 (also catalogued by Ring as the Wired Video Doorbell Pro) is a mains-powered smart video doorbell. According to Ring's published specifications, the unit captures 1536p HD+ video in a square aspect ratio with a 150-degree horizontal and 150-degree vertical field of view, providing a head-to-toe perspective of visitors and doorstep deliveries. The manufacturer states that the doorbell uses radar-assisted 3D Motion Detection to calculate distance and trace visitor approaches via its Bird's Eye View feature. Audio hardware includes two-way talk with noise cancellation alongside preset quick reply options. For networking, the doorbell connects to both 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz dual-band Wi-Fi, transmitting video and notifications through the Ring app.

## Who it suits
This doorbell suits households built around Amazon Alexa. Ring notes that the Pro 2 can announce visitors on Echo speakers and automatically pull up live video feeds on Echo Show displays. Because the unit contains no internal battery, it suits property owners who want continuous power without having to remove the doorbell to recharge battery cells. It is also well suited to front entrances close to busy roads or pedestrian footpaths, where Ring's radar-based distance boundaries help minimise false alerts.

Households operating primarily on Apple Home or Google Home should look elsewhere, as Ring limits voice assistant integration to Alexa. Renters should note that installing the unit involves either running an external cable to an indoor socket or drilling mounting hardware into exterior cladding, which may conflict with tenancy agreements without prior landlord consent. Additionally, Ring states that reviewing recorded clips and activating person or package alerts requires an active Ring Protect subscription, making this doorbell unsuitable for buyers who want out-of-the-box local storage.

## Australian notes
In Australia, the Ring Video Doorbell Pro 2 is backed by a 12-month manufacturer warranty. According to Ring, the doorbell requires 16 to 24 VAC (10VA to 40VA) or a compatible 24 VDC transformer. Most Australian homes do not have existing low-voltage doorbell transformers. If you choose to hardwire the device using a DIN-rail transformer in your switchboard, that is 240V work, which is generally work for a licensed electrician; check your state's electrical safety regulator. Alternatively, Ring produces an Australian plug-in adapter kit that connects directly to a standard internal power point. When positioning the camera, check your state or territory's surveillance device and privacy laws on recording neighbouring properties and public footpaths.

The platform you choose matters more than any individual device you buy. It determines how you control everything, how automations behave, what happens when the internet drops, and how much of your home's data leaves the country.

Since Matter arrived, this decision is thankfully less permanent than it used to be — most new hardware works with all of them. But the platforms themselves remain genuinely different, and the differences show up daily.

Here are the five real options.

## Apple Home

**Best for:** households already on iPhone who value privacy and reliability over flexibility.

Apple Home is the most conservative option and, for many people, the most pleasant to live with. Automations run locally on a home hub — an Apple TV or HomePod — which means they keep working when your connection drops. Data handling is the strictest of the major platforms, and camera footage processed through HomeKit Secure Video is analysed on your own hub rather than in a cloud somewhere.

The interface is clean and the reliability is good. Once something is set up, it tends to stay working.

The trade-offs are real. The automation engine is the least capable of the five — conditional logic is limited, and anything genuinely clever requires a third-party app like Controller or Shortcuts gymnastics. Siri remains the weakest voice assistant for general questions. And if anyone in the household is on Android, they are locked out entirely.

**Australian note:** everything Apple Home does is available here, and HomeKit Secure Video's local processing is a genuine advantage if you are uneasy about camera footage leaving the country.

## Google Home

**Best for:** Android households, and anyone who uses voice control constantly.

Google Assistant is still the best voice assistant for natural language. It handles compound requests, follow-up questions and general knowledge better than Siri or Alexa, and it understands Australian accents and place names well.

Google Home's automation capability sits in the middle of the pack — better than Apple's, less powerful than SmartThings or Home Assistant. The app has been through several redesigns of varying quality, which long-time users will have opinions about.

The main concern is philosophical: Google's business is data, and a Google smart home means Google knows a great deal about your household's patterns. How much that bothers you is a personal call, but it should be a conscious one.

**Australian note:** Nest hardware availability here has been patchy over the years, with some products launching late or not at all. Check current local availability rather than assuming a US announcement applies.

## Amazon Alexa

**Best for:** the widest device compatibility and the cheapest entry point.

Alexa supports more third-party devices than anything else, largely because Amazon spent years making integration easy for manufacturers. If you have an obscure brand, Alexa is the most likely to support it.

Echo speakers are also routinely the cheapest smart speakers available, and go on sale constantly. Filling a house with Alexa speakers costs meaningfully less than doing the same with HomePods or Nest speakers.

The downsides: the app is cluttered, "Skills" are an inconsistent mess of quality, and Alexa's routines are functional but not sophisticated. Alexa also leans heavily on the cloud, so an outage takes more with it than on Apple.

**Australian note:** Alexa's Australian localisation is good, though some US-only features and Skills never make it here.

## Samsung SmartThings

**Best for:** people who want serious automation without running their own server.

SmartThings occupies a useful middle ground. Its automation engine is genuinely capable, it supports Zigbee, Z-Wave and Thread natively in the hub hardware, and it acts as a Matter controller and bridge. If you want to bring a mixed collection of older devices into a modern setup, SmartThings does it with less effort than anything else.

It has had a turbulent history — a major platform migration frustrated long-time users, and Samsung's commitment has waxed and waned. It is in a good state now, but it carries more platform risk than Apple, Google or Amazon.

**Australian note:** confirm any Z-Wave hub you buy is the Australia/New Zealand radio variant. Z-Wave frequencies are region-specific, and a US-model hub will not communicate with Australian Z-Wave devices.

## Home Assistant

**Best for:** anyone who wants actual control, and does not mind earning it.

Home Assistant is open-source software you run yourself, on a Raspberry Pi, a mini PC or a purpose-built box. It supports effectively everything, integrates with devices the commercial platforms ignore, and its automation engine is limited only by your patience.

Everything runs locally. Your data stays in your house. When the NBN goes down, your home keeps working. For Australians on flaky connections — and for anyone who has watched a cloud service get discontinued — this is the strongest argument for it.

The cost is time. Setup takes an afternoon, not ten minutes. Updates occasionally break things. You will read documentation. Household members who did not choose this may find it frustrating when something needs fixing and only you know how.

A pragmatic pattern many people land on: run Home Assistant as the brain, and bridge everything to Apple Home or Google Home so the rest of the household gets a simple app and voice control that always works.

## How to actually decide

**Start with the phones.** If everyone is on iPhone, Apple Home is a strong default. If the household is mixed, avoid Apple Home as the primary — it will strand someone.

**Be honest about tinkering.** Home Assistant is enormously rewarding if you enjoy the process. If configuring things is a chore rather than a hobby, you will resent it, and a half-maintained Home Assistant is worse than a well-used Google Home.

**Think about internet reliability.** If your connection is unreliable — and plenty of Australian connections are — weight local processing heavily. There is nothing more irritating than a light switch that does not work because a data centre overseas is having a bad day.

**Consider who else lives there.** The best platform is the one everyone can use. A technically superior setup that only one person understands is a liability, not an achievement.

## You are not as locked in as you think

Thanks to Matter's multi-admin support, a single device can belong to several platforms at once. You can genuinely try Google Home and Apple Home side by side with the same hardware before committing.

That is a significant change from a few years ago, and it means this decision — while important — is no longer irreversible. Start with the one that fits your household, and adjust as you learn what you actually want.
